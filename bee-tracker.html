How the numbers are calculated

Positions are smoothed with a moving average over the window above, within stretches where the bee is continuously detected (gaps up to the "longest gap" are bridged; longer gaps split the track and are not counted). Tracked time is the time inside those stretches. Distance sums the steps between smoothed positions; mean speed is distance ÷ tracked time; moving is the share of tracked time with speed above the threshold. Zone time is the share of tracked time the centroid was inside each zone; visits count entries. Merges count episodes when this bee touched another and had to be split from a shared blob; reacquired counts times it was lost for a while and matched again. After either, the bee's identity may have swapped with another bee, so with several unmarked bees, use the All bees row, which does not depend on identity.

Detection

s

Tracking compares each frame with a picture of the arena without bees. Best: Capture empty arena before the bees go in. Otherwise Learn while bees move takes the per-pixel median over several seconds, which only works if no bee stays in one spot for half that time. Capture again if the lighting or camera moves.

The background is the per-pixel median of frames spread through the analysis range, so bees that keep moving disappear from it. A bee that sits still for most of the recording becomes part of the background and is missed.

Each step holds both outputs at the levels you set for that long; the whole protocol is played by the DAQ's own clock. Start trial starts it on the trial's first frame, and stopping the trial turns both LEDs off. A light check box over an LED in view measures how late the camera sees each change.

Compare recordings

p-values: Mann–Whitney U for two groups, Kruskal–Wallis for three or more. They are exploratory. Bees that share an arena are not independent, and their identities can swap, so compare recordings rather than bees unless each arena held one bee or the bees were marked.
